Orthonormal Basis Normal Matrix

Definition. A square matrix $A \in \mathbb{C}^{n \times n}$ is called normal if it commutes with its conjugate transpose:

$$A A^* = A^* A,$$

where $A^ = \bar{A}^\top$. The class of normal matrices includes Hermitian matrices ($A^ = A$), skew-Hermitian matrices ($A^ = -A$), and unitary matrices ($A A^ = I$). A collection of vectors $\{v_1, v_2, \ldots, v_n\}$ in $\mathbb{C}^n$ forms an orthonormal basis if $\langle v_i, v_j \rangle = \delta_{ij}$ for all $i, j$, where $\delta_{ij}$ is the Kronecker delta.

Worked Example. Consider the real symmetric matrix

$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ 1 & 3 \end{pmatrix}.$$

Since $A = A^\top$, it is Hermitian and hence normal. Its characteristic polynomial is $(\lambda - 3)^2 - 1 = 0$, giving eigenvalues $\lambda_1 = 2$ and $\lambda_2 = 4$. The corresponding eigenvectors are $u_1 = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(-1, 1)^\top$ and $u_2 = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(1, 1)^\top$. One verifies $\langle u_1, u_2 \rangle = 0$ and $\|u_i\| = 1$, so $\{u_1, u_2\}$ is an orthonormal basis of $\mathbb{R}^2$ composed entirely of eigenvectors of $A$.

Key Theorem (Spectral Theorem for Normal Matrices). A matrix $A \in \mathbb{C}^{n \times n}$ is normal if and only if there exists a unitary matrix $U$ such that $A = U \Lambda U^$, where $\Lambda = \mathrm{diag}(\lambda_1, \ldots, \lambda_n)$ contains the eigenvalues of $A$.*

Proof sketch. By Schur's theorem, every square matrix is unitarily triangularizable: $A = U T U^$ for some upper triangular $T$. If $A$ is normal, then $TT^ = T^*T$. A direct computation on diagonal entries shows that $T$ must be diagonal, so $T = \Lambda$. $\square$

The columns of $U$ form an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors — the central structural fact that makes normal matrices analytically tractable.

Lab Cell (SymPy).

from sympy import Matrix, GramSchmidt

A = Matrix([[3, 1], [1, 3]])
eigvecs = [v for _, _, vs in A.eigenvects() for v in vs]
ortho = GramSchmidt(eigvecs, orthonormal=True)
U = Matrix(ortho).T          # columns are the orthonormal eigenvectors
print("U =", U)
print("Diagonalization check:", U.T * A * U)  # should be diagonal
