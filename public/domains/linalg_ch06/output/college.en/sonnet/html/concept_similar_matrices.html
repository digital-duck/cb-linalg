Similar Matrices

Definition. Two $n \times n$ matrices $A$ and $B$ are called similar if there exists an invertible matrix $P$ such that

$$B = P^{-1}AP.$$

The matrix $P$ is called a change-of-basis matrix, and the transformation $A \mapsto P^{-1}AP$ is a similarity transformation. Similarity is an equivalence relation: it is reflexive ($A = I^{-1}AI$), symmetric, and transitive. Geometrically, $A$ and $B$ represent the same linear transformation $T : \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^n$, but expressed with respect to different ordered bases. The columns of $P$ carry the new basis vectors written in the old coordinate system.

Worked Example. Let

$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 4 & 1 \\ 2 & 3 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad P = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -2 \end{pmatrix}.$$

Compute $P^{-1}$. Since $\det P = -3$,

$$P^{-1} = \frac{1}{-3}\begin{pmatrix} -2 & -1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 2/3 & 1/3 \\ 1/3 & -1/3 \end{pmatrix}.$$

Then

$$B = P^{-1}AP = \begin{pmatrix} 5 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}.$$

The result $B$ is diagonal, revealing the eigenvalues $\lambda_1 = 5$ and $\lambda_2 = 2$ of $A$ directly on its main diagonal. The columns of $P$ are precisely the corresponding eigenvectors.

Key Theorem. Similar matrices share all of the following invariants: (i) the same characteristic polynomial $\det(\lambda I - A)$; (ii) the same eigenvalues with identical algebraic and geometric multiplicities; (iii) the same determinant; (iv) the same trace; and (v) the same rank. None of these quantities depends on the choice of basis. Conversely, two diagonalizable matrices are similar if and only if they have the same eigenvalues with the same multiplicities.

Proof sketch. For the characteristic polynomial: $\det(\lambda I - B) = \det(\lambda I - P^{-1}AP) = \det(P^{-1}(\lambda I - A)P) = \det(\lambda I - A)$, using multiplicativity of the determinant. All remaining invariants follow because they are expressible in terms of the characteristic polynomial. $\square$

Lab Cell.

from sympy import Matrix, symbols

A = Matrix([[4, 1], [2, 3]])
P = Matrix([[1, 1], [1, -2]])

B = P.inv() * A * P
print("B =", B)                          # diagonal form
print("char poly A:", A.charpoly())
print("char poly B:", B.charpoly())      # must match
print("eigenvals A:", A.eigenvals())
