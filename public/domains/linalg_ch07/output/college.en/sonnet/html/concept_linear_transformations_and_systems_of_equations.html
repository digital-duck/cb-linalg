Linear Transformations And Systems Of Equations

Definition. Let $V$ and $W$ be vector spaces over a field $\mathbb{F}$. A function $T: V \to W$ is called a linear transformation if it satisfies two properties for all $\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v} \in V$ and all scalars $c \in \mathbb{F}$:

$$T(\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v}) = T(\mathbf{u}) + T(\mathbf{v}) \qquad \text{(additivity)}$$ $$T(c\mathbf{u}) = c\,T(\mathbf{u}) \qquad \text{(homogeneity)}$$

When $V = \mathbb{R}^n$ and $W = \mathbb{R}^m$, every linear transformation is represented by left-multiplication by an $m \times n$ matrix $A$: that is, $T(\mathbf{x}) = A\mathbf{x}$. The system of equations $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$ therefore asks: does the vector $\mathbf{b}$ lie in the image of $T$?

Worked Example. Consider the linear system

$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 \end{pmatrix}, \quad \mathbf{b} = \begin{pmatrix} 5 \\ 6 \end{pmatrix}.$$

The transformation $T(\mathbf{x}) = A\mathbf{x}$ maps $\mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^2$. To solve $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$, we row-reduce the augmented matrix $[A \mid \mathbf{b}]$:

$$\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 5 \\ 3 & 4 & 6 \end{pmatrix} \xrightarrow{R_2 - 3R_1} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 5 \\ 0 & -2 & -9 \end{pmatrix} \xrightarrow{-\frac{1}{2}R_2} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 5 \\ 0 & 1 & \frac{9}{2} \end{pmatrix} \xrightarrow{R_1 - 2R_2} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & -4 \\ 0 & 1 & \frac{9}{2} \end{pmatrix}.$$

The unique solution is $\mathbf{x} = (-4,\; \tfrac{9}{2})^\top$, confirming that $\mathbf{b}$ is in the image of $T$.

Key Theorem (Rank–Nullity). For a linear transformation $T: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^m$ with matrix $A$,

$$\dim(\ker T) + \dim(\operatorname{im} T) = n,$$

where $\ker T = \{\mathbf{x} : A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{0}\}$ is the null space and $\operatorname{im} T$ is the column space of $A$. The system $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$ has a solution if and only if $\mathbf{b} \in \operatorname{im} T$, and the solution is unique if and only if $\ker T = \{\mathbf{0}\}$ — equivalently, $\operatorname{rank}(A) = n$.

Lab Cell.

from sympy import Matrix

A = Matrix([[1, 2], [3, 4]])
b = Matrix([5, 6])

# Solve Ax = b
x = A.solve(b)
print("Solution:", x)

# Inspect structure
print("Null space:", A.nullspace())
print("Column space:", A.columnspace())
print("Rank:", A.rank())

Running this cell verifies the worked example and illustrates the rank–nullity relationship directly: since $\operatorname{rank}(A) = 2 = n$, the null space is trivial and the solution is unique.
