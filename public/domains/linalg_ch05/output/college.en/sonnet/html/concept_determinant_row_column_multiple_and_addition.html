Determinant Row Column Multiple And Addition

Definition. Let $A$ be an $n \times n$ matrix with determinant $\det(A)$. Two elementary row (or column) operations concern us here:

  • Scalar multiple: Replace row $i$ by $k \cdot R_i$ for some scalar $k \neq 0$. This multiplies $\det(A)$ by $k$.
  • Row addition: Replace row $i$ by $R_i + k \cdot R_j$ for $i \neq j$. This leaves $\det(A)$ unchanged.

    • The same rules hold verbatim when applied to columns.

    Worked Example. Let

    $$A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 4 & 3 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \det(A) = (2)(3) - (1)(4) = 2.$$

    Operation 1 — scalar multiple: Multiply $R_1$ by $5$:

    $$A' = \begin{pmatrix} 10 & 5 \\ 4 & 3 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \det(A') = (10)(3) - (5)(4) = 10 = 5 \cdot \det(A). \checkmark$$

    Operation 2 — row addition: Starting again from $A$, replace $R_2$ by $R_2 - 2R_1$:

    $$A'' = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \det(A'') = (2)(1) - (1)(0) = 2 = \det(A). \checkmark$$

    Key Theorem. Let $E$ be an elementary matrix corresponding to adding $k$ times row $j$ to row $i$ ($i \neq j$). Then $\det(EA) = \det(A)$.

    Proof sketch. Expand $\det(EA)$ along row $i$. After the operation, row $i$ equals $R_i + k R_j$. By multilinearity of the determinant,

    $$\det(EA) = \det(\ldots, R_i, \ldots) + k\,\det(\ldots, R_j, \ldots).$$

    The second determinant has two identical rows ($R_j$ appears twice), so it equals zero. Hence $\det(EA) = \det(A)$. $\blacksquare$

    This theorem is the engine behind Gaussian elimination: one can reduce any matrix to triangular form using only row-addition operations, none of which alter the determinant, plus at most $n$ scalar-multiple steps whose net effect is tracked by a running product.

    Lab Cell (SymPy).

    from sympy import Matrix, det

A = Matrix([[2, 1], [4, 3]])
A_scaled = A.elementary_row_op('n->kn', row=0, k=5)
A_added  = A.elementary_row_op('n->n+km', row=1, k=-2, row2=0)

print(det(A), det(A_scaled), det(A_added))  # 2, 10, 2

    Running this cell confirms both rules simultaneously: the scaled matrix yields $5 \cdot \det(A) = 10$, while the row-addition matrix preserves $\det(A) = 2$.
