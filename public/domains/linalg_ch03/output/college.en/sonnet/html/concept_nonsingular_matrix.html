Nonsingular Matrix

Definition. An $n \times n$ matrix $A$ with entries in a field $\mathbb{F}$ is called nonsingular (or invertible) if there exists an $n \times n$ matrix $B$ such that

$$AB = BA = I_n,$$

where $I_n$ denotes the $n \times n$ identity matrix. The matrix $B$, which is unique when it exists, is called the inverse of $A$ and is written $A^{-1}$. A matrix that is not nonsingular is called singular.

The condition of nonsingularity is equivalent to several other fundamental properties. Most directly, $A$ is nonsingular if and only if $\det(A) \neq 0$. This single scalar criterion underlies the theoretical importance of the concept: the determinant measures the factor by which $A$ scales $n$-dimensional volume, and a zero determinant signals that the transformation collapses space into a lower-dimensional subspace, making inversion impossible.

Worked Example. Consider the matrix

$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 5 & 3 \end{pmatrix}.$$

Computing the determinant: $\det(A) = (2)(3) - (1)(5) = 6 - 5 = 1 \neq 0$, so $A$ is nonsingular. Its inverse is given by the standard $2 \times 2$ formula

$$A^{-1} = \frac{1}{\det(A)}\begin{pmatrix} 3 & -1 \\ -5 & 2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & -1 \\ -5 & 2 \end{pmatrix}.$$

One verifies directly that $AA^{-1} = I_2$.

Key Theorem (Invertible Matrix Theorem). For an $n \times n$ matrix $A$, the following statements are equivalent:

  • $A$ is nonsingular.
  • $\det(A) \neq 0$.
  • The system $Ax = b$ has a unique solution for every $b \in \mathbb{F}^n$.

    • Proof sketch. Conditions (1) and (3) are equivalent because, when $A^{-1}$ exists, the unique solution is $x = A^{-1}b$; conversely, unique solvability for every $b$ forces the existence of such an inverse. The determinant criterion (2) is established by showing that row reduction to $I_n$ is possible precisely when no pivot is zero, which occurs if and only if $\det(A) \neq 0$.

    Lab Cell (SymPy).

    from sympy import Matrix

A = Matrix([[2, 1], [5, 3]])
print("det(A) =", A.det())        # 1  → nonsingular
print("A⁻¹ =")
print(A.inv())
print("Verification A·A⁻¹ =")
print(A * A.inv())                 # should give Identity matrix
