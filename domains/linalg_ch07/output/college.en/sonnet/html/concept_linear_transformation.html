Linear Transformation

Definition. Let $V$ and $W$ be vector spaces over a field $\mathbb{F}$. A function $T: V \to W$ is called a linear transformation (or linear map) if it satisfies two properties for all vectors $\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v} \in V$ and all scalars $c \in \mathbb{F}$:

$$T(\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v}) = T(\mathbf{u}) + T(\mathbf{v}) \quad \text{(additivity)}$$ $$T(c\,\mathbf{u}) = c\,T(\mathbf{u}) \quad \text{(homogeneity)}$$

These two conditions are equivalent to the single statement $T(c\,\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v}) = c\,T(\mathbf{u}) + T(\mathbf{v})$. Geometrically, a linear transformation preserves the operations that define vector space structure: it maps lines through the origin to lines through the origin, and it respects scalar stretching.

Worked Example. Define $T: \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^2$ by $T(x, y) = (2x + y,\; x - 3y)$. To verify linearity, let $\mathbf{u} = (x_1, y_1)$ and $\mathbf{v} = (x_2, y_2)$:

$$T(\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v}) = T(x_1+x_2,\; y_1+y_2) = \bigl(2(x_1+x_2)+(y_1+y_2),\; (x_1+x_2)-3(y_1+y_2)\bigr)$$

which equals $T(\mathbf{u}) + T(\mathbf{v})$. Homogeneity follows similarly. The associated matrix is

$$[T] = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & -3 \end{pmatrix},$$

so $T(\mathbf{v}) = [T]\mathbf{v}$ for every $\mathbf{v} \in \mathbb{R}^2$.

Theorem (Matrix Representation). Every linear transformation $T: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^m$ is uniquely represented by an $m \times n$ matrix $A$ such that $T(\mathbf{x}) = A\mathbf{x}$. The $j$-th column of $A$ is $T(\mathbf{e}_j)$, where $\{\mathbf{e}_1, \dots, \mathbf{e}_n\}$ is the standard basis of $\mathbb{R}^n$. Proof sketch: Linearity forces $T(\mathbf{x}) = T(\sum x_j \mathbf{e}_j) = \sum x_j T(\mathbf{e}_j)$, which is exactly the matrix–vector product with $A = [T(\mathbf{e}_1) \mid \cdots \mid T(\mathbf{e}_n)]$. $\square$

Lab Cell.

from sympy import Matrix, symbols

x, y = symbols('x y')
A = Matrix([[2, 1], [1, -3]])
v = Matrix([x, y])

Tv = A * v
print("T(v) =", Tv)          # [2x + y, x - 3y]

# Verify on a concrete vector
v0 = Matrix([3, -1])
print("T([3,-1]) =", A * v0)  # [5, 6]
