Inner Product

Definition. Let $V$ be a vector space over a field $\mathbb{F}$ (either $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$). An inner product on $V$ is a function $\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle : V \times V \to \mathbb{F}$ satisfying, for all $\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v}, \mathbf{w} \in V$ and $\alpha \in \mathbb{F}$:

  • Conjugate symmetry: $\langle \mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v} \rangle = \overline{\langle \mathbf{v}, \mathbf{u} \rangle}$
  • Linearity in the first argument: $\langle \alpha\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{w}, \mathbf{v} \rangle = \alpha\langle \mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v} \rangle + \langle \mathbf{w}, \mathbf{v} \rangle$
  • Positive definiteness: $\langle \mathbf{v}, \mathbf{v} \rangle \geq 0$, with equality if and only if $\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{0}$

    • A vector space equipped with an inner product is called an inner product space. Over $\mathbb{R}$, conjugate symmetry reduces to ordinary symmetry $\langle \mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v} \rangle = \langle \mathbf{v}, \mathbf{u} \rangle$.

    Worked Example. Consider $\mathbb{R}^3$ with the standard (Euclidean) inner product $\langle \mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v} \rangle = \mathbf{u}^\top \mathbf{v} = \sum_{i=1}^{3} u_i v_i$. Take $\mathbf{u} = (1, 2, -1)^\top$ and $\mathbf{v} = (3, 0, 2)^\top$. Then

    $$\langle \mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v} \rangle = (1)(3) + (2)(0) + (-1)(2) = 3 + 0 - 2 = 1.$$

    The induced norm is $\|\mathbf{v}\| = \sqrt{\langle \mathbf{v}, \mathbf{v} \rangle} = \sqrt{9 + 0 + 4} = \sqrt{13}$. The angle $\theta$ between the two vectors satisfies $\cos\theta = \langle \mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v} \rangle / (\|\mathbf{u}\|\|\mathbf{v}\|)$, providing a coordinate-free measure of geometric orientation.

    Key Theorem (Cauchy–Schwarz Inequality). For any $\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v}$ in an inner product space,

    $$|\langle \mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v} \rangle|^2 \leq \langle \mathbf{u}, \mathbf{u} \rangle \cdot \langle \mathbf{v}, \mathbf{v} \rangle.$$

    Proof sketch. For $\mathbf{v} \neq \mathbf{0}$, define $\mathbf{w} = \mathbf{u} - \frac{\langle \mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v} \rangle}{\langle \mathbf{v}, \mathbf{v} \rangle}\mathbf{v}$. Positive definiteness gives $\langle \mathbf{w}, \mathbf{w} \rangle \geq 0$; expanding this expression and rearranging yields the inequality.

    Lab Cell (SymPy).

    from sympy import symbols, Matrix, sqrt, acos, pi

u = Matrix([1, 2, -1])
v = Matrix([3, 0,  2])

ip   = u.dot(v)                       # inner product
norm_u = sqrt(u.dot(u))
norm_v = sqrt(v.dot(v))
theta = acos(ip / (norm_u * norm_v))

print(f"⟨u, v⟩ = {ip}")
print(f"‖v‖    = {norm_v}")
print(f"θ      = {theta} rad  ≈ {float(theta.evalf()):.4f} rad")
