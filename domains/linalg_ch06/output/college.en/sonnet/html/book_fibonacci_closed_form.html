Concept Book: Fibonacci Closed Form

What the Closed Form Achieves

The Fibonacci sequence is defined recursively: $F_0 = 0$, $F_1 = 1$, and $F_n = F_{n-1} + F_{n-2}$ for $n \geq 2$. This recurrence is elegant but computationally expensive — to find $F_{100}$, a naïve implementation must compute every predecessor. The closed form, known as Binet's formula, dissolves this dependency entirely:

$$F_n = \frac{\varphi^n - \psi^n}{\sqrt{5}}, \qquad \varphi = \frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}, \quad \psi = \frac{1-\sqrt{5}}{2}.$$

Here $\varphi$ is the golden ratio, approximately $1.6180$, and $\psi \approx -0.6180$. Because $|\psi| < 1$, the term $\psi^n / \sqrt{5}$ decays toward zero, so for large $n$ the formula reduces to $F_n \approx \varphi^n / \sqrt{5}$, rounded to the nearest integer.

Worked Example

Compute $F_7$ without recursion. Substituting $n = 7$:

$$F_7 = \frac{\varphi^7 - \psi^7}{\sqrt{5}} = \frac{(1.6180\ldots)^7 - (-0.6180\ldots)^7}{\sqrt{5}} \approx \frac{29.034 - (-0.034)}{2.236} = \frac{29.068}{2.236} \approx 13.$$

Indeed $F_7 = 13$. The irrational quantities cancel perfectly, yielding an integer — a remarkable arithmetic coincidence that the derivation via eigenvalues of the companion matrix makes inevitable.

Key Theorem

Theorem (Binet, 1843). Let $\varphi$ and $\psi$ be the two roots of the characteristic equation $x^2 - x - 1 = 0$. Then for all $n \geq 0$,

$$F_n = \frac{\varphi^n - \psi^n}{\varphi - \psi}.$$

Proof sketch. The general solution to the linear recurrence $F_n = F_{n-1} + F_{n-2}$ is $F_n = A\varphi^n + B\psi^n$. Applying initial conditions $F_0 = 0$ and $F_1 = 1$ yields $A = 1/\sqrt{5}$ and $B = -1/\sqrt{5}$, giving Binet's formula. $\square$

The derivation is a direct application of the theory of linear recurrences with constant coefficients — the same machinery that governs differential equations and discrete-time dynamical systems.

Lab Cell (SymPy)

from sympy import sqrt, Rational, simplify, symbols

n = symbols('n', positive=True, integer=True)
phi = (1 + sqrt(5)) / 2
psi = (1 - sqrt(5)) / 2

def binet(k):
    return simplify((phi**k - psi**k) / sqrt(5))

for k in range(10):
    print(f"F_{k} = {binet(k)}")

Running this cell confirms that SymPy's symbolic arithmetic recovers exact integers, illustrating that the irrationalities cancel algebraically — not merely numerically.

Invitation

The closed form is not merely a computational shortcut; it is a lens. The golden ratio $\varphi$ appears in the geometry of pentagons, in the limiting ratio of successive Fibonacci numbers, and — perhaps most strikingly — in the spectral analysis of biological growth patterns. If you wish to follow one thread to its source, turn next to the connection between $\varphi$ and continued fractions: you will find that $\varphi = 1 + \tfrac{1}{1+\tfrac{1}{1+\cdots}}$ is the "most irrational" of all irrationals, and that this extremal property is precisely why Fibonacci numbers appear wherever nature optimizes packing.
