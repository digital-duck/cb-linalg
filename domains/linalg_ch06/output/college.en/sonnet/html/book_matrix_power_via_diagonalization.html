Matrix Power via Diagonalization

Computing the $n$-th power of a matrix naively requires $n - 1$ matrix multiplications, each costing $O(k^3)$ operations for a $k \times k$ matrix. When $n$ is large — as it is in virtually every sequence, dynamical system, and network diffusion problem — this brute-force approach is computationally ruinous. Diagonalization eliminates the bottleneck entirely.

Recall that if $A = P D P^{-1}$, where $D = \operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \ldots, \lambda_k)$, then:

$$A^n = P D^n P^{-1}, \qquad D^n = \operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1^n, \, \lambda_2^n, \, \ldots, \, \lambda_k^n).$$

Raising a diagonal matrix to the $n$-th power costs exactly $k$ scalar exponentiations. The entire computation collapses from $O(n k^3)$ to $O(k^3)$ — independent of $n$. This is the natural endpoint of the diagonalization thread: the machinery built to understand eigenvalues pays its dividend by turning iteration into closed-form evaluation.

Connection to the Fibonacci Closed Form. Define the state vector $\mathbf{v}_n = \bigl(F_{n+1},\, F_n\bigr)^\top$. The Fibonacci recurrence $F_{n+1} = F_n + F_{n-1}$ is exactly the linear map:

$$\mathbf{v}_n = A\,\mathbf{v}_{n-1}, \qquad A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}.$$

Applying the diagonalization formula gives $\mathbf{v}_n = A^n \mathbf{v}_0 = P D^n P^{-1} \mathbf{v}_0$. The eigenvalues of $A$ are the golden ratio $\varphi = \tfrac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}$ and its conjugate $\hat\varphi = \tfrac{1-\sqrt{5}}{2}$. Reading off the second component of $A^n \mathbf{v}_0$ produces Binet's formula immediately:

$$F_n = \frac{\varphi^n - \hat\varphi^n}{\sqrt{5}}.$$

No recursion, no iteration — a single arithmetic expression evaluated once.

Lab Cell (SymPy)

from sympy import Matrix, sqrt, simplify, symbols
n = symbols('n', positive=True, integer=True)
A = Matrix([[1, 1], [1, 0]])
P, D = A.diagonalize()
An = P * D**n * P.inv()
F_n = simplify(An[1, 0])   # lower-left entry gives F_n
print(F_n)                  # Binet's formula

The result printed by SymPy is exactly Binet's formula, derived mechanically from the eigenstructure. This is the promise of diagonalization made concrete: a formula that ancient mathematicians could only guess at falls out of a handful of linear-algebra steps.
