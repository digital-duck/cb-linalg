Row Operations

Definition. Let $A$ be an $m \times n$ matrix over a field $\mathbb{F}$. A row operation is any one of three invertible transformations applied to the rows of $A$:

  • Row swap $(R_i \leftrightarrow R_j)$: interchange rows $i$ and $j$.
  • Row scaling $(R_i \leftarrow cR_i)$: multiply every entry of row $i$ by a nonzero scalar $c \in \mathbb{F}^*$.
  • Row addition $(R_i \leftarrow R_i + cR_j,\ i \neq j)$: add $c$ times row $j$ to row $i$.

    • Each operation corresponds to left-multiplication by an elementary matrix $E$ (obtained by applying the same operation to the identity $I_m$), so the transformed matrix is $EA$. Because every elementary matrix is invertible, row operations preserve the row space and, crucially, the solution set of the linear system $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$.

    Worked Example. Consider

    $$A = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & 3 \\ 2 & 4 & 0 \\ -1 & 2 & 5 \end{pmatrix}.$$

    Step 1 $(R_1 \leftrightarrow R_2)$: move the nonzero pivot into position,

    $$\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 4 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 3 \\ -1 & 2 & 5 \end{pmatrix}.$$

    Step 2 $(R_3 \leftarrow R_3 + \tfrac{1}{2}R_1)$: eliminate the $-1$ below the pivot,

    $$\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 4 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 3 \\ 0 & 4 & 5 \end{pmatrix}.$$

    Step 3 $(R_3 \leftarrow R_3 - 4R_2)$: eliminate the entry below the second pivot,

    $$\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 4 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 3 \\ 0 & 0 & -7 \end{pmatrix}.$$

    The result is upper-triangular (row echelon form), achieved by three row operations.

    Key Theorem (Equivalence of row operations). Two matrices $A$ and $B$ are row equivalent if and only if $B = E_k \cdots E_2 E_1 A$ for some sequence of elementary matrices. Row-equivalent matrices have identical row spaces and the same rank. In particular, the reduced row echelon form (RREF) of a matrix is unique, regardless of the sequence of operations used.

    Proof sketch. Each elementary matrix $E_i$ is invertible, so the product $P = E_k \cdots E_1$ is invertible. The row space of $PA$ equals $P$ applied to the row space of $A$, but invertible linear maps preserve the span within the ambient space — hence rank is invariant. Uniqueness of RREF follows from the uniqueness of the leading-$1$ pivot positions, proved by induction on column index.

    Lab Cell (SymPy).

    from sympy import Matrix

A = Matrix([[0, 1, 3],
            [2, 4, 0],
            [-1, 2, 5]])

# Reduced row echelon form and pivot columns
rref, pivots = A.rref()
print("RREF:\n", rref)
print("Pivot columns:", pivots)

    Running this cell returns the RREF with pivot columns $(0, 1, 2)$, confirming that $A$ has full rank $3$.
