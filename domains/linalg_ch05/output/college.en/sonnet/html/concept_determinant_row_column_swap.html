Determinant Row Column Swap

Definition. Let $A$ be an $n \times n$ matrix. If $B$ is obtained from $A$ by interchanging any two rows (or any two columns), then

$$\det(B) = -\det(A).$$

This sign-reversal property is one of the three axioms that uniquely characterize the determinant function, and every other determinant rule can be derived from it together with multilinearity and normalization.

Worked Example. Consider the matrix

$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \det(A) = (1)(4) - (2)(3) = -2.$$

Swap the two rows to form

$$B = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 4 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \det(B) = (3)(2) - (4)(1) = 2.$$

Indeed $\det(B) = -\det(A) = 2$. Now swap instead the two columns of $A$:

$$C = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 4 & 3 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \det(C) = (2)(3) - (1)(4) = 2 = -\det(A).$$

Both operations produce the same sign flip, confirming that rows and columns are treated symmetrically by the determinant.

Key Theorem. (Antisymmetry implies vanishing for repeated rows.) If any two rows (or two columns) of $A$ are identical, then $\det(A) = 0$.

Proof sketch. Let $B$ be obtained by swapping those two identical rows. On one hand the swap gives $\det(B) = -\det(A)$; on the other hand $B = A$ because the rows are the same, so $\det(B) = \det(A)$. Therefore $\det(A) = -\det(A)$, which forces $\det(A) = 0$. $\square$

A direct corollary is that if the rows of $A$ are linearly dependent (one row is a linear combination of others), then $\det(A) = 0$, establishing the link between determinants and invertibility.

Lab Cell (SymPy).

from sympy import Matrix

A = Matrix([[1, 2], [3, 4]])
B = Matrix([[3, 4], [1, 2]])   # rows swapped
C = Matrix([[2, 1], [4, 3]])   # columns swapped

print(A.det(), B.det(), C.det())
# Expected output: -2  2  2

Run this cell to verify numerically that each elementary swap negates the determinant, and experiment with larger matrices to confirm the theorem holds in general.
