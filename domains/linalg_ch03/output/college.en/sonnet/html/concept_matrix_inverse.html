Matrix Inverse

Definition. Let $A$ be an $n \times n$ square matrix. A matrix $B$ is called the inverse of $A$, written $A^{-1}$, if

$$AB = BA = I_n,$$

where $I_n$ is the $n \times n$ identity matrix. A matrix possessing an inverse is called invertible (or non-singular); one that does not is called singular.

Invertibility is not guaranteed. The fundamental criterion is that $A$ is invertible if and only if $\det(A) \neq 0$. Equivalently, $A$ is invertible if and only if its columns are linearly independent, or if and only if the homogeneous system $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{0}$ has only the trivial solution.

Worked Example. Find the inverse of

$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 5 & 3 \end{pmatrix}.$$

Compute $\det(A) = (2)(3) - (1)(5) = 1 \neq 0$, so $A^{-1}$ exists. For a $2 \times 2$ matrix $\begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix}$, the inverse formula gives

$$A^{-1} = \frac{1}{\det(A)}\begin{pmatrix} d & -b \\ -c & a \end{pmatrix} = \frac{1}{1}\begin{pmatrix} 3 & -1 \\ -5 & 2 \end{pmatrix}.$$

Verify: $AA^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 5 & 3 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 3 & -1 \\ -5 & 2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}. \checkmark$

Theorem (Uniqueness of the Inverse). If $A$ is invertible, its inverse $A^{-1}$ is unique.

Proof sketch. Suppose both $B$ and $C$ satisfy $AB = BA = I$ and $AC = CA = I$. Then $B = BI = B(AC) = (BA)C = IC = C$. $\square$

Two further properties follow directly from the definition:

$$\left(A^{-1}\right)^{-1} = A, \qquad (AB)^{-1} = B^{-1}A^{-1}.$$

The reversal of order in the product rule is essential and is often a source of error.

Lab Cell (SymPy).

from sympy import Matrix

A = Matrix([[2, 1], [5, 3]])
A_inv = A.inv()
print("A_inv =", A_inv)
print("Verification A @ A_inv =", A * A_inv)   # should be identity
print("det(A) =", A.det())

Running this cell confirms $A^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & -1 \\ -5 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$ symbolically and verifies $AA^{-1} = I_2$.
