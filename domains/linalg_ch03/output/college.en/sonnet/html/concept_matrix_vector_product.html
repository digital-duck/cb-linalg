Matrix Vector Product

Definition. Let $A$ be an $m \times n$ matrix with entries $a_{ij}$, and let $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^n$ be a column vector. The matrix–vector product $A\mathbf{x}$ is the vector $\mathbf{b} \in \mathbb{R}^m$ whose $i$-th entry is

$$b_i = \sum_{j=1}^{n} a_{ij}\, x_j, \quad i = 1, \ldots, m.$$

Equivalently, $A\mathbf{x}$ is a linear combination of the columns of $A$, weighted by the entries of $\mathbf{x}$:

$$A\mathbf{x} = x_1 \mathbf{a}_1 + x_2 \mathbf{a}_2 + \cdots + x_n \mathbf{a}_n,$$

where $\mathbf{a}_j$ denotes the $j$-th column of $A$. This column-space interpretation is central to understanding systems of linear equations.

Worked Example. Consider

$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ 3 & 4 \end{pmatrix}, \quad \mathbf{x} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}.$$

Applying the row-dot-product rule:

$$b_1 = (2)(1) + (-1)(2) = 0, \qquad b_2 = (3)(1) + (4)(2) = 11.$$

Hence $A\mathbf{x} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 11 \end{pmatrix}$. Alternatively, via the column view: $1 \cdot \begin{pmatrix}2\\3\end{pmatrix} + 2 \cdot \begin{pmatrix}-1\\4\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}2-2\\3+8\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}0\\11\end{pmatrix}$, confirming both interpretations agree.

Key Theorem (Linearity of the Matrix–Vector Product). For any $m \times n$ matrix $A$, vectors $\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v} \in \mathbb{R}^n$, and scalar $c \in \mathbb{R}$:

$$A(\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v}) = A\mathbf{u} + A\mathbf{v}, \qquad A(c\,\mathbf{u}) = c\,(A\mathbf{u}).$$

Proof sketch. Both identities follow immediately from the distributivity and homogeneity of the summation $\sum_j a_{ij} x_j$ over its inputs. This pair of properties characterizes $A$ as a linear map from $\mathbb{R}^n$ to $\mathbb{R}^m$, establishing the fundamental correspondence between matrices and linear transformations. $\blacksquare$

Lab Cell (SymPy).

from sympy import Matrix

A = Matrix([[2, -1], [3, 4]])
x = Matrix([1, 2])

b = A * x          # matrix–vector product
print("A * x =", b)

# Verify linearity: A(u + v) == Au + Av
u = Matrix([1, 0])
v = Matrix([0, 1])
assert A * (u + v) == A * u + A * v
print("Linearity confirmed.")

Running this cell prints $\begin{pmatrix}0\\11\end{pmatrix}$ and verifies the linearity property symbolically, bridging the algebraic definition with direct computation.
