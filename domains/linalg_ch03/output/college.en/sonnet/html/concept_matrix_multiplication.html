Matrix Multiplication

Definition. Let $A$ be an $m \times n$ matrix and $B$ be an $n \times p$ matrix. The matrix product $C = AB$ is the $m \times p$ matrix whose $(i,j)$-entry is defined by

$$c_{ij} = \sum_{k=1}^{n} a_{ik}\, b_{kj}, \quad 1 \le i \le m,\; 1 \le j \le p.$$

The product is defined precisely when the number of columns of $A$ equals the number of rows of $B$. In terms of dot products, each entry $c_{ij}$ is the inner product of the $i$-th row of $A$ with the $j$-th column of $B$.

Worked Example. Let

$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 \end{pmatrix}, \quad B = \begin{pmatrix} 5 & 6 \\ 7 & 8 \end{pmatrix}.$$

Computing entry by entry:

$$c_{11} = (1)(5)+(2)(7) = 19, \quad c_{12} = (1)(6)+(2)(8) = 22,$$ $$c_{21} = (3)(5)+(4)(7) = 43, \quad c_{22} = (3)(6)+(4)(8) = 50.$$

Hence $AB = \begin{pmatrix} 19 & 22 \\ 43 & 50 \end{pmatrix}$. Notice that computing $BA$ yields $\begin{pmatrix} 23 & 34 \\ 31 & 46 \end{pmatrix} \ne AB$, illustrating that matrix multiplication is in general non-commutative.

Theorem (Associativity and Distributivity). For conformable matrices $A$, $B$, $C$ and scalar $\lambda$:

  • $(AB)C = A(BC)$ (associativity),
  • $A(B+C) = AB + AC$ (left distributivity),
  • $(\lambda A)B = \lambda(AB)$ (scalar homogeneity).

    • Proof sketch. Each identity reduces to verifying that the $(i,j)$-entry on both sides equals the same finite sum of products $a_{ik}b_{kl}c_{lj}$; rearranging the order of summation establishes equality. $\square$

    A critical consequence is that matrix multiplication represents composition of linear maps: if $T_A : \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^m$ and $T_B : \mathbb{R}^p \to \mathbb{R}^n$ are the maps encoded by $A$ and $B$ respectively, then $AB$ encodes $T_A \circ T_B$.

    from sympy import Matrix, symbols

A = Matrix([[1, 2], [3, 4]])
B = Matrix([[5, 6], [7, 8]])

C = A * B          # matrix product
print("AB =", C)
print("BA =", B * A)
print("Commutative?", A * B == B * A)
