Diagonal Matrix

Definition. A square matrix $D \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ is called a diagonal matrix if every off-diagonal entry is zero; that is, $d_{ij} = 0$ whenever $i \neq j$. Such a matrix is written compactly as $D = \operatorname{diag}(d_1, d_2, \ldots, d_n)$, where $d_k = d_{kk}$ denotes the $k$-th diagonal entry. The zero matrix and the identity matrix $I_n = \operatorname{diag}(1, 1, \ldots, 1)$ are canonical special cases.

Worked Example. Let $D = \operatorname{diag}(3, -1, 4)$. Explicitly,

$$D = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 4 \end{pmatrix}.$$

Computing the product $D\mathbf{x}$ for $\mathbf{x} = (2, 5, 1)^\top$ gives

$$D\mathbf{x} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 \cdot 2 \\ (-1) \cdot 5 \\ 4 \cdot 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 6 \\ -5 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix}.$$

Each coordinate of $\mathbf{x}$ is scaled independently by the corresponding diagonal entry, confirming the geometric interpretation: $D$ stretches or reflects each coordinate axis without mixing them.

Key Theorem. Diagonal matrices form a commutative subalgebra of $\mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$. Specifically, if $D = \operatorname{diag}(d_1, \ldots, d_n)$ and $E = \operatorname{diag}(e_1, \ldots, e_n)$, then:

  • $D + E = \operatorname{diag}(d_1 + e_1, \ldots, d_n + e_n)$,
  • $DE = \operatorname{diag}(d_1 e_1, \ldots, d_n e_n)$,
  • $DE = ED$.

    • Proof sketch. For entry $(i,j)$, the $(i,j)$ entry of $DE$ equals $\sum_{k=1}^n d_{ik} e_{kj}$. Since $d_{ik} = 0$ for $k \neq i$ and $e_{kj} = 0$ for $k \neq j$, the only surviving term occurs at $k = i = j$, yielding $d_{ii} e_{ii}$. Symmetry of this expression in $D$ and $E$ establishes commutativity. $\square$

    A further consequence is that $D^{-1} = \operatorname{diag}(d_1^{-1}, \ldots, d_n^{-1})$ whenever all $d_k \neq 0$, and that $\det(D) = \prod_{k=1}^n d_k$.

    Lab Cell (SymPy).

    from sympy import diag, Matrix, symbols

d1, d2, d3 = symbols('d1 d2 d3')
D = diag(d1, d2, d3)
E = diag(3, -1, 4)

print("D =", D)
print("det(E) =", E.det())          # -12
print("E^{-1} =", E.inv())
print("DE == ED:", D * E == E * D)  # True

    Running this cell verifies the theorem symbolically and reinforces that diagonal matrix arithmetic reduces to elementwise scalar operations.
