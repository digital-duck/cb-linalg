Diagonalization Characterization

Definition. A square matrix $A \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ is said to be diagonalizable if there exists an invertible matrix $P \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ and a diagonal matrix $D = \operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \ldots, \lambda_n)$ such that

$$A = P D P^{-1}.$$

Equivalently, $A$ is diagonalizable if and only if $\mathbb{R}^n$ possesses a basis consisting entirely of eigenvectors of $A$. The columns of $P$ are those eigenvectors, and the corresponding diagonal entries of $D$ are the associated eigenvalues.

Worked Example. Consider the matrix

$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 4 & 1 \\ 2 & 3 \end{pmatrix}.$$

The characteristic polynomial is $\det(A - \lambda I) = (4 - \lambda)(3 - \lambda) - 2 = \lambda^2 - 7\lambda + 10 = (\lambda - 2)(\lambda - 5)$, yielding eigenvalues $\lambda_1 = 2$ and $\lambda_2 = 5$. Solving $(A - 2I)\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{0}$ gives eigenvector $\mathbf{v}_1 = (-1, 2)^\top$; solving $(A - 5I)\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{0}$ gives $\mathbf{v}_2 = (1, 1)^\top$. Since these two vectors are linearly independent, we set

$$P = \begin{pmatrix} -1 & 1 \\ 2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad D = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 5 \end{pmatrix},$$

and verify that $AP = PD$, confirming $A = PDP^{-1}$.

Theorem (Diagonalizability Criterion). A matrix $A \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ is diagonalizable if and only if for each eigenvalue $\lambda_i$, the geometric multiplicity (dimension of the eigenspace $\ker(A - \lambda_i I)$) equals the algebraic multiplicity (multiplicity of $\lambda_i$ as a root of the characteristic polynomial). In particular, any matrix with $n$ distinct eigenvalues is automatically diagonalizable, since each geometric multiplicity is at least one and cannot exceed the corresponding algebraic multiplicity of one.

Proof sketch. Eigenvectors from distinct eigenspaces are linearly independent. When all multiplicities match, the eigenspaces together span $\mathbb{R}^n$, furnishing the required eigenvector basis. The converse follows by counting dimensions.

Lab Cell.

from sympy import Matrix, pprint

A = Matrix([[4, 1], [2, 3]])
P, D = A.diagonalize()   # raises if not diagonalizable
pprint(P)
pprint(D)
assert A == P * D * P.inv()

Running this cell prints $P$ and $D$ and silently passes the assertion, confirming the decomposition symbolically.
