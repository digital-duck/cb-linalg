Determinant Expansion About Columns

Definition. Let $A = [a_{ij}]$ be an $n \times n$ matrix. The cofactor of the entry $a_{ij}$ is defined as

$$C_{ij} = (-1)^{i+j} M_{ij},$$

where $M_{ij}$ is the $(n-1)\times(n-1)$ minor obtained by deleting row $i$ and column $j$ from $A$. The determinant of $A$ may be computed by expanding along any column $j$:

$$\det(A) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} a_{ij}\, C_{ij} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} (-1)^{i+j}\, a_{ij}\, M_{ij}.$$

This is the cofactor expansion along column $j$. The choice of column is free; every column yields the same value $\det(A)$, a consequence of the multilinearity and alternating properties of the determinant.

Worked Example. Compute $\det(A)$ by expanding along column 2, where

$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 0 & 2 \\ 1 & 4 & -1 \\ 5 & 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix}.$$

Column 2 contains entries $a_{12}=0$, $a_{22}=4$, $a_{32}=0$. Two of the three terms vanish immediately:

$$\det(A) = 0 \cdot C_{12} + 4 \cdot C_{22} + 0 \cdot C_{32} = 4\,C_{22}.$$

The cofactor is $C_{22} = (-1)^{2+2} M_{22}$. Deleting row 2 and column 2 gives

$$M_{22} = \det\begin{pmatrix} 3 & 2 \\ 5 & 3 \end{pmatrix} = (3)(3)-(2)(5) = -1.$$

Therefore $\det(A) = 4 \cdot (+1)(-1) = -4$.

Expanding along column 1 or column 3 yields the same value, confirming column-independence.

Key Theorem. (Column Independence of Cofactor Expansion.) For any $n \times n$ matrix $A$ and any fixed column index $j \in \{1,\dots,n\}$,

$$\det(A) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} a_{ij}\, C_{ij}.$$

Moreover, the alien cofactor identity states that expanding entries of column $k \neq j$ against cofactors of column $j$ gives zero:

$$\sum_{i=1}^{n} a_{ik}\, C_{ij} = 0 \quad (k \neq j).$$

This pair of identities is the foundation for Cramer's Rule and matrix inversion via the adjugate.

Lab Cell (SymPy).

from sympy import Matrix, symbols, expand

A = Matrix([[3, 0, 2],
            [1, 4, -1],
            [5, 0, 3]])

# Full determinant
print("det(A) =", A.det())

# Manual column-2 expansion (0-indexed: column index 1)
expansion = sum(A[i, 1] * A.cofactor(i, 1) for i in range(3))
print("Column-2 expansion =", expand(expansion))

Running this cell prints det(A) = -4 for both lines, verifying that the cofactor expansion along column 2 reproduces the determinant exactly.
