Determinant By Row Reduction

Definition. The determinant of an $n \times n$ matrix $A$, denoted $\det(A)$ or $|A|$, is a scalar that encodes fundamental geometric and algebraic properties of the linear map represented by $A$. Row reduction provides a computationally efficient method for evaluating $\det(A)$ by reducing $A$ to an upper-triangular form, exploiting the fact that the determinant of a triangular matrix equals the product of its diagonal entries.

Three elementary row operations affect the determinant in precisely controlled ways:

  • Row swap ($R_i \leftrightarrow R_j$): multiplies $\det$ by $-1$.
  • Row scaling ($R_i \leftarrow cR_i$, $c \neq 0$): multiplies $\det$ by $c$.
  • Row addition ($R_i \leftarrow R_i + kR_j$, $i \neq j$): leaves $\det$ unchanged.

    • By tracking these operations during Gaussian elimination, one recovers $\det(A)$ from the resulting triangular matrix.

    Worked Example. Let

    $$A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 & 3 \\ 4 & 5 & 6 \\ 0 & 2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}.$$

    Step 1: $R_2 \leftarrow R_2 - 2R_1$ (no determinant change):

    $$\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 & 3 \\ 0 & 3 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}.$$

    Step 2: $R_3 \leftarrow R_3 - \tfrac{2}{3}R_2$ (no determinant change):

    $$U = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 & 3 \\ 0 & 3 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}.$$

    Since no swaps or scalings were performed, $\det(A) = \det(U) = 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 1 = 6$.

    Key Theorem (Determinant via LU Decomposition). Every invertible matrix $A$ admits a factorisation $PA = LU$, where $P$ is a permutation matrix, $L$ is unit lower-triangular, and $U$ is upper-triangular. Then

    $$\det(A) = \frac{\det(U)}{\det(P)} = (-1)^s \prod_{i=1}^{n} u_{ii},$$

    where $s$ is the number of row swaps encoded in $P$. If $A$ is singular, at least one diagonal entry of $U$ is zero, so $\det(A) = 0$, confirming non-invertibility.

    Lab Cell.

    from sympy import Matrix, det, symbols

A = Matrix([[2, 1, 3],
            [4, 5, 6],
            [0, 2, 1]])

# Built-in determinant (uses Bareiss / fraction-free row reduction internally)
print("det(A) =", det(A))

# Manual verification: row-reduce and track pivot product
_, pivot_list = A.LUdecomposition()
print("Pivots:", pivot_list)

    Running this cell confirms $\det(A) = 6$ and reveals the pivots $2, 3, 1$ extracted during elimination. Row reduction thus transforms an $O(n!)$ cofactor expansion into an $O(n^3)$ algorithm, making determinant computation tractable for large matrices.
