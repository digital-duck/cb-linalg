Matrix Power Via Diagonalization

Definition. A square matrix $A \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ is diagonalizable if there exists an invertible matrix $P$ and a diagonal matrix $D$ such that

$$A = P D P^{-1}, \quad D = \operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \ldots, \lambda_n),$$

where $\lambda_1, \ldots, \lambda_n$ are the eigenvalues of $A$ and the columns of $P$ are the corresponding eigenvectors. This factorization is powerful because diagonal matrices are trivially raised to any power: $(D^k)_{ii} = \lambda_i^k$. Consequently, computing $A^k$ for large $k$ reduces to scalar exponentiation rather than repeated matrix multiplication.

Derivation. Observe that

$$A^2 = (PDP^{-1})(PDP^{-1}) = PD(P^{-1}P)DP^{-1} = PD^2P^{-1}.$$

By induction, for any positive integer $k$,

$$\boxed{A^k = P D^k P^{-1}}, \quad D^k = \operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1^k, \lambda_2^k, \ldots, \lambda_n^k).$$

This reduces an $O(n^3 k)$ chain of matrix products to a single similarity transformation at cost $O(n^3)$, independent of $k$.

Worked Example. Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$. The eigenvalues satisfy $\det(A - \lambda I) = (3-\lambda)(2-\lambda) = 0$, giving $\lambda_1 = 3$, $\lambda_2 = 2$. The eigenvectors are $v_1 = (1, 0)^\top$ and $v_2 = (-1, 1)^\top$, so

$$P = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \quad P^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}.$$

For $k = 5$, $D^5 = \operatorname{diag}(243, 32)$, and therefore

$$A^5 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 243 & 0 \\ 0 & 32 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 243 & 211 \\ 0 & 32 \end{pmatrix}.$$

Key Theorem. An $n \times n$ matrix with $n$ linearly independent eigenvectors is diagonalizable. In particular, any matrix with $n$ distinct eigenvalues satisfies this condition.

Lab Cell (SymPy).

from sympy import Matrix
A = Matrix([[3, 1], [0, 2]])
P, D = A.diagonalize()          # returns P, D such that A = P D P**-1
print(P * D**5 * P**-1)         # verify A^5 symbolically
