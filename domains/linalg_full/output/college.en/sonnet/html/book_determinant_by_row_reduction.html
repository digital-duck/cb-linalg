What Row-Reduction Achieves

The determinant of a square matrix $A \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ encodes a single scalar that answers the most fundamental question in linear algebra: does $A$ have an inverse, and if so, how does it scale volume? The naive definition via cofactor expansion requires $O(n!)$ arithmetic operations, rendering it useless for any $n$ of practical size. Row reduction closes this gap entirely.

Definition. Let $A$ be reduced to upper-triangular form $U$ by a sequence of elementary row operations. If $k$ row swaps are performed and each scaling operation multiplies a row by a nonzero constant $c_i$, then

$$\det(A) = \frac{(-1)^k}{\prod_i c_i} \prod_{j=1}^{n} u_{jj},$$

where $u_{jj}$ are the diagonal entries of $U$. In the common case where only addition-of-multiple-of-row operations are used (which preserve the determinant), this simplifies to $\det(A) = (-1)^k \prod_{j=1}^n u_{jj}$.

This reduces computation to $O(n^3)$—the same cost as solving a linear system—making determinants tractable for matrices of thousands of rows.

Worked Example

Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 & 3 \\ 4 & 5 & 6 \\ 0 & 2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$. Eliminating below the first pivot:

$$R_2 \leftarrow R_2 - 2R_1 \implies \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 & 3 \\ 0 & 3 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}.$$

Then $R_3 \leftarrow R_3 - \tfrac{2}{3}R_2$:

$$U = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 & 3 \\ 0 & 3 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}.$$

No row swaps occurred, so $\det(A) = 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 1 = 6$.

Key Theorem

Theorem (Multiplicativity). For $A, B \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$,

$$\det(AB) = \det(A)\,\det(B).$$

Proof sketch. Every invertible matrix is a product of elementary matrices, each with a known determinant effect; the result follows by induction on the LU factorization. When $A$ is singular its row reduction produces a zero diagonal entry, giving $\det(A) = 0$ and confirming $AB$ is also singular.

Lab Cell

from sympy import Matrix, symbols

A = Matrix([[2, 1, 3],
            [4, 5, 6],
            [0, 2, 1]])

# Row-reduction path
_, U, swaps = A.LUdecomposition()          # P A = L U
print("Diagonal of U:", [U[i,i] for i in range(3)])
print("det via product:", (-1)**len(swaps) * U.det())
print("SymPy reference:", A.det())

Row reduction is not merely a computational shortcut; it is the proof that invertibility, volume scaling, and linear independence are facets of a single numerical witness. Every application you will encounter—change of variables in multivariable integration, stability analysis of differential equations, eigenvalue characteristic polynomials—ultimately asks whether a matrix is invertible and by what factor it distorts space. You now possess the efficient, exact answer. Choose any one of these domains and trace how $\det(A) \neq 0$ becomes the fulcrum on which the entire theory turns.
